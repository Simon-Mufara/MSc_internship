Key Experimental Design Gaps & Platform Responses
🔬
1. Tissue Dissociation Protocol — Direct Impact on QC Thresholds
Enzymatic dissociation versus mechanical dissociation produces systematically different stress response gene profiles. This affects which genes appear in the HVG selection and which clusters you identify.
→ Computational parameter design must account for upstream wet-lab choices to avoid confounding.
📦
2. Batch Effects from Library Preparation — The Silent Confounder
Library preparation batches introduce systematic technical variation indistinguishable from biological signal without metadata.
→ Batch correction must precede downstream analysis; metadata is prerequisite data.
🧬
3. Biological vs Technical Replicates — What Your Statistics Actually Test
Technical replicates vs biological replicates require different statistical models. Pseudobulk methods are critical for multi-donor designs.
→ Platform enforces replicate structure declaration and applies appropriate models.
❄️
4. Sample Preservation & RNA Quality — What RIN Scores Tell You
Fresh vs cryopreserved samples produce systematically different profiles. Standard QC thresholds will over-filter cryopreserved samples.
→ Adaptive QC thresholds based on preservation method prevent loss of valid data.
🔢
5. Cell Loading Density — Doublet Rate Is Experimental, Not Computational
Doublet rates in 10x Chromium data are a direct function of cell loading. Over-loading increases doublet rates degrading analysis.
→ Platform calculates expected doublet rate from loading density.
⏱️
6. Sequencing Depth — Reads per Cell Is a Wet-Lab Budget Decision
Low-depth libraries produce sparse count matrices where rare transcripts are missed. This trade-off is made at sequencing submission.
→ Platform flags depth-quality issues before downstream analysis.
